## What it is

<!-- synthesis -->

The guide's plan records treat what an opening costs as three separate numbers, and the step of putting them side by side is the guide's own. The first is acquisition spend, the one that appears in a budget line. SHRM's 2025 benchmarking survey of 2,371 members put the median cost per hire at $1,200 for nonexecutive roles, down 27 percent since 2017, and $10,625 for executive roles, up 113 percent, with the figure defined as agency fees, job boards, referral costs, travel, relocation, recruiter pay and benefits, and systems, divided by hires [196]. The clock that spend runs against is a median time to fill of about 45 days for executive and nonexecutive roles alike, with 41 percent of organizations citing rising candidate ghosting [43].

The second number is the vacancy itself. Across 27 case studies that excluded executives and physicians, the median cost of replacing a worker was about 21 percent of annual salary, 16 percent for jobs paying $30,000 or less and up to 213 percent for very highly paid roles, and only 2 of the 11 papers reviewed counted indirect costs at all [180]. At the level of the unit, a meta-analysis of 694 effect sizes from 82 studies found turnover rates correlated about −.22 with customer satisfaction and production efficiency, −.15 with profit margin and +.14 with error and loss rates, the same for voluntary and total turnover, while staffing levels and straight pay were not significantly related to turnover [181]. In German administrative records of 34,000 unexpected worker deaths, the firm raised the wages and retention of the remaining workers in the same occupation, coworkers in other occupations lost when a high-skilled or specialized worker died, and replacement costs were substantial and larger in thin labor markets and for specialized skills [193]. The people covering an empty seat carry a cost of their own that does not show as hours: stress and exhaustion predict quitting at ρ .21, role conflict and role ambiguity at .15 each and work–life conflict at .19, while hours of workload as such barely register at −.10 [178].

The third number is cash, and it comes from the contract and the payment rules. A time-and-materials or labor-hour contract pays a fixed hourly rate for labor that meets a specified category's qualifications and is performed on the contract, and hours worked by employees who do not meet them are not paid without the contracting officer's authorization [98] [99]. Labor that is not charged to a contract is an indirect cost, accumulated in pools and allocated across the firm's other work through its rates [148]. Where the contract sets no date a proper invoice is due to be paid 30 days after the start of the payment period, an improper invoice is returned within 7 days and interest is paid automatically on late payments [202]; agencies are directed to provide accelerated payments to small business contractors with a goal of 15 days, and primes that receive them pass the acceleration to small subcontractors within 15 days, although the goal creates no new Prompt Payment Act rights [201].

Two measurements sit at the edges, and one dispute runs through them. Where output could be counted per worker, among windshield installers at one U.S. autoglass company, output was 53 percent higher after one year than at hire, with pay profiles much flatter than output for the first 18 months; the work is manual, so the slope does not transfer to technical staff, only the shape [157]. Before the ramp can begin the hire has to arrive: among 157,154 accepted entry-level offers at one telecommunications employer, 4.1 percent never showed up, and each additional week between acceptance and start raised the odds of a no-show by about a fifth (OR 1.22) [144]. The dispute is the multiple. A trade white paper puts direct turnover costs at 50 to 60 percent of salary and total costs at 90 to 200 percent and names no study for either figure [195], while the measured median across the case studies is 21 percent [180]; the guide carries the disagreement as the controversy `cost-of-turnover`, with the unit-level correlations and the specialist case as the positions in between [181] [193].

## The key idea in one picture

The cost-of-a-hire figure is three columns for one opening, drawn to different scales and labeled as such. The first column is acquisition spend, marked at the 2025 medians of $1,200 and $10,625 [196]. The second is the vacancy, marked at the 21 percent case-study median with the 16 percent and 213 percent ends of the range as faint ticks [180] and annotated with the unit-level correlation of −.22 with customer satisfaction [181]. The third is drawn as a timeline: payroll running from the start date, shaded as indirect cost until the first chargeable hour [148], then the 30-day default to payment with the 15-day goal marked inside it [202] [201]. Above the second column a dashed ghost column rises to the 90 to 200 percent multiple, labeled as resting on no named study [195], and a thin line across the top traces output at 53 percent above the hire-date level after one year [157].

## How this guide uses it

The three parts are the `plan` stage's economics and the constraint `c-requisition-economics-unseen`, which sits inside the controversy `cost-of-turnover` and names the levers `l-all-in-acquisition-budget`, `l-vacancy-cost-estimate` and `l-hiring-ahead-exposure-cap` [196] [180] [202]. The cash part reappears at the clear-start stage as `c-no-chargeable-work-before-access` with the lever `l-bridge-work-plan` [148] [99]; the ramp is `c-ramp-to-productivity-exceeds-plan` at the onboard stage [157]; the strain on those who cover is `c-vacancy-overload-cascade` at the retain stage [178]; and the specialist case is `c-knowledge-concentrated-in-few-people` at the handover stage, with `l-cross-training-and-documented-handoffs` [193].

The gates that fix the arithmetic are `tm-labor-category-rates` and `prompt-payment-and-accelerated-payment` [99] [202] [201], and the indicators that carry the dated figures are `ind-shrm-cost-per-hire-2025`, `ind-shrm-time-to-fill-2025`, `ind-turnover-cost-share-of-salary`, `ind-collective-turnover-performance-2013`, `ind-newcomer-output-ramp-first-year` and `ind-new-hire-ghosting-rate-2025` [196] [43] [180] [181] [157] [144], with the enabler `shrm-recruiting-benchmarks` pointing at the survey behind the first two.
