## What it is

The question of which hiring methods predict later job performance has a current standard answer, the 2022 re-estimation by Sackett, Zhang, Berry and Lievens, which took the databases behind the earlier meta-analyses and recalculated each method's operational validity, the correlation between a score at hiring and performance on the job [1]. The revised table puts structured interviews first at .42, then job-knowledge tests at .40, empirically keyed biodata at .38, work samples at .33, and cognitive ability and integrity tests at .31 each; unstructured interviews sit at .19 and years of job experience at .07 [1]. Read as a ranking, the table says that a scored conversation about the work, a test of what the job requires, or a sample of the work itself tells a firm far more than an unscored conversation or a count of years on a résumé [1].

These figures are lower than the ones selection textbooks carried for two decades. Schmidt and Hunter's 1998 summary of 85 years of research put general mental ability at .51, the structured interview at .51, work samples at .54 and the unstructured interview at .38 [4]. The 2022 authors showed that the earlier work had applied range-restriction corrections derived from predictive studies, where applicants are tested and then hired before their performance is known, to the concurrent studies of people already on the job that dominate the databases, and that this overcorrected; without that correction the estimates fall by .10 to .20, the rank order of the strong predictors largely holds, and cognitive ability moves well down the list [1]. Whether concurrent studies need no correction at all is disputed in print [2], and the guide carries the dispute as the controversy `cognitive-ability-validity`; the ordering that matters to a small firm, structure above judgment, is common to both sides [1] [4].

Each validity in the revised table is paired with a Black–White standardized difference, d, so that the cost of a predictor is visible beside its benefit: cognitive ability carries d = .79, work samples .67, job-knowledge tests .54, assessment centers .52, years of experience .49, unstructured interviews .32, structured interviews .23 and integrity tests .10 [1]. The cognitively loaded measures with the most validity show the largest group differences, and the structured interview is the method that pairs high validity with a modest one, with a caveat the guide keeps beside it: two behavioral interviews used as a first screen showed d of .36 and .56 once range restriction was corrected [18]. The authors say the d values mix applicant, incumbent and national samples and are offered to locate predictors as having large, modest or minimal group differences [1].

How the evidence is combined matters as much as which evidence is collected. When the same predictor information is combined by formula instead of by judgment, prediction of job performance rises from r = .28 to .44 across nine comparisons, and the authors conclude that experts collect information well and combine it poorly [7]. Field evidence from fifteen service-sector firms that introduced a pre-employment test points the same way: managers who hired against the test's recommendation made worse hires on average, and the test scores better identified the workers who would stay [10]. Applicants have a ranking of their own. Across 86 samples and 48,750 people, interviews (3.84 on a five-point scale) and work samples (3.63) were the best-liked procedures, honesty tests (2.47) and graphology (1.76) the least liked, and those perceptions predict attraction to the employer, intention to accept an offer and willingness to recommend it [14].

## The key idea in one picture

A scatter plot with operational validity on the horizontal axis and the Black–White d on the vertical, one labeled point per method from the 2022 table: structured interviews sit far to the right and low (.42, .23), cognitive ability to the right of center and high (.31, .79), work samples between them (.33, .67), and unstructured interviews and years of experience near the left edge (.19 and .07) [1]. The methods that are both predictive and modest in group difference gather in the lower right, where the guide's select-stage levers sit; a dotted second marker for the structured interview at d = .36 and .56 keeps the first-screen caveat on the page [18].

## How this guide uses it

The Uniform Guidelines are the frame the figures sit in. An employer keeps selection rates by sex and by race or ethnic group, and a group's rate below four-fifths of the highest group's is generally regarded as evidence of adverse impact, qualified by statistical and practical significance and by small numbers [31]; a procedure with adverse impact needs criterion-related, content or construct validity evidence tied to important elements of job performance, with cutoffs set to normal expectations of proficiency and more evidence for ranking than for pass/fail use [32]; the eighteen sections were adopted jointly by the EEOC, the Civil Service Commission, DOL and DOJ in 1978 [30]. The gate `ugesp` carries these rules and `eeoc-selection-procedures` the EEOC's plain map of them, including the employer's continuing responsibility for a vendor's test [33].

In the records, the table is the evidence behind the select-stage constraints `c-unstructured-judgement`, `c-keyword-screen-rejects-qualified`, where years of experience at .07 is the proxy a keyword screen encodes, and `c-validity-diversity-tradeoff`, and behind the levers `l-structured-interview`, `l-work-sample-or-knowledge-test` and `l-mechanical-scoring`, whose effect lines carry the .42, the .40 and .33, and the .44 against .28 [1] [7]. The override finding is a diagnostic in `c-unstructured-judgement`, a candidate who scored well on a work sample or test and was rejected after an interview with no documented reason [10], and the applicant-reaction ranking is part of what the pattern `withdraw-mid-process` points at [14]. At the plan stage the same table supplies one of the three definitions of "qualified" in `c-firm-and-customer-define-qualified-differently`, beside the labor category's education and experience line and the customer reviewer's standard [1] [7] [99].
