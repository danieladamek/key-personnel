- Nothing from the private source matrix is cited as fact; every one of its rows and categories is a hypothesis in `hypotheses.yaml`. No firm is named anywhere in the pack or the app.

## What sweep 1 found, in one paragraph

Structure beats judgement by a wide margin and the evidence is unusually consistent: structured interviews (.42), job-knowledge tests (.40) and work samples (.33) predict performance while unstructured interviews (.19) and years of experience (.07) barely do; combining evidence by formula rather than feel recovers more than a third of the achievable validity; managers who override a valid test hire worse. The cost side is also clear: the most valid tests carry the largest group differences, applicants like interviews and work samples and dislike personality and honesty tests, and slow or burdensome processes lose people. The gates have changed since 2025: the EEOC withdrew its AI guidance while four states and one city now impose notice, audit and retention duties; the Fair Chance Act times criminal-history questions for contractors; and DOJ has fined employers for "citizens only" postings on export-controlled work. Five of the author's seven select-stage hypotheses survive with qualifications; the category itself dissolves into the stages.

## What sweep 2 found, in one paragraph

Referral is the best-evidenced source (10–30% lower quit rates across nine firms; higher interview and offer odds) and the narrowest, because networks are segregated and reproduce the founders' composition. What a posting draws is set by its title and its pay figure (within a title, 10% more pay draws 7.7% more applicants), and most competing postings now show pay; the words matter too, since masculine-coded wording lowers women's appeal and "recent college graduate" is an unlawful age term. The announced shift to skills-based hiring changed who was hired by less than one in 700 because screens did not change. Reach has changed character: relocation is under two percent of job seekers and workers price a hybrid option at 8% of pay, which a cleared or onsite employer cannot offer. Small firms report the thinnest pools (87% few or no qualified applicants), and the cleared pool is closed by the sponsorship rule. The contractor EEO framework resets on 26 October 2026 when the EO 11246 regulations, the Internet Applicant rule among them, are removed. Five of the eight A-rows survive with qualifications and two are supported outright.

## What sweep 3 found, in one paragraph

The offer has a ceiling the firm did not set. Under a T&M or labor-hour contract each labor category's hourly rate already contains the wage, indirect cost and profit and pays only for labor that meets the category's qualifications, so the salary is a residue; the rate is fixed at award while wages rose 3.1 percent and benefits 3.8 percent in the year to June 2026, and a schedule contractor may ask for at most three increases a period inside a window. The comparison the candidate makes is wider than salary: benefits are 30 percent of compensation cost, 97 percent of large firms offer health coverage against 59 percent of small ones, and workers price a work-from-home option at about 8 percent of pay and schedule predictability at about 20 percent. Every market-rate offer lands on the incumbents: external hires started 18 percent above internal promotees in the one firm measured and performed worse, and people who learn they earn less than the unit median search for other jobs while those above gain nothing. Transparency cuts both ways in the evidence, with right-to-discuss laws lowering wages about 2 percent and posting laws raising them 1.3 to 3.6 percent; stating that pay is negotiable closes the gender negotiation gap; about twenty states bar the salary-history question, and the Ninth Circuit bars prior pay as an Equal Pay Act defence. Bonuses on cost-type work are allowable only under a plan that predates the work, and no study of whether counteroffers or sign-on bonuses work was found. GAO and the Court of Federal Claims disagree on the duty to disclose a key person who has become unavailable before award. Three of the four offer-stage D-rows are supported and the category splits, its economics going to sweep 7.

## What sweep 4 found, in one paragraph

The stage runs on other people's clocks and the rules say so plainly. A firm can request a clearance only for an employee a classified contract needs, never to build a bench, and only once someone else has sponsored its facility clearance; a candidate investigated before employment must start within 45 days of the grant. DCSA's industry cases averaged 197 days for a Secret and 243 for a Top Secret in the third quarter of FY2026, on a reporting basis that changed that quarter, against targets of 40 and 75 and a median time to fill of 45; GAO found the older figures understated, and nobody publishes how often a contractor applicant gets an interim. Eligibility is not access: the credential needs a sponsor, a favourable fingerprint check and a submitted investigation, a transferred clearance a five-day reciprocity decision that agencies do not track, and a cyber role its certificate on day one. The wait itself has a measured cost for the first time in the pack: among 157,154 accepted offers at one employer, each extra week between acceptance and start raised the odds of a no-show by about a fifth, and a referral or good communication roughly halved them. Payroll in the interval is the firm's indirect cost, which raises the rates that cap its salaries. The start carries statutory clocks of its own (I-9, E-Verify, FCRA), the DFARS drug-testing clause has quietly been reserved, and a foreign national waits on an export authorization or a visa whose $100,000 payment a court has stopped. Three of the six C-rows are supported outright, three with qualifications, and the category becomes the stage.

## What sweep 5 found, in one paragraph

Onboarding has an evidence base the matrix never had a category for. Across 183 newcomer samples, structured tactics correlate .24 to .40 with adjustment and the social ones, an experienced insider beside the hire and affirmation of what they bring, .29 to .47; social acceptance then carries performance (.35) and, weakly, staying (−.12). At one firm with no orientation a quarter of newcomers left within a year and insider role models roughly halved the odds; support from supervisor and team falls off week by week through the first ninety days, and an undermining supervisor triples the hazard of leaving. The ramp is real where output can be counted (+53 percent in year one, with pay much flatter), and the labor-category rule that assumes the finished worker is enforced by audit (101 of 112 unqualified employees improperly charged; about $35 million). On the develop side the economics say why a small firm trains at all, because it knows what the trained worker can do better than the market does and keeps a productivity–wage wedge (0.6 against 0.3 percent) while the person stays; learning from knowledgeable coworkers is itself four to nine percent of pay, which is what mentoring costs in senior hours. Training transfers when it looks like the work and the supervisor expects it used. The instruments for holding the investment are narrowing: nearly a tenth of workers had a repayment agreement in 2020, California bars most such terms for contracts after January 1, 2026, Colorado limits them to distinct training at cost declining over two years, the NLRB memoranda are rescinded and the FTC rule is gone; paying for education retains mostly through who it attracts. The tax code's $5,250 exclusion is indexed from 2027 with the loan-repayment option made permanent. Two F-rows are supported outright, six with qualifications, and the category splits into two stages.

## What sweep 6 found, in one paragraph

Retention has a ranked list and pay is not at the top of it. Across 316 studies, wanting to leave (ρ .56) and looking (.40) are the strongest predictors, then commitment, fit and satisfaction near −.3, embeddedness (−.26), the supervisor (−.24) and the alternatives a person sees (.23); pay sits at −.17 and hours of workload at −.10, while stress, role conflict and work–life conflict run .15 to .21, which is how a covered vacancy actually costs people. Pay moves people through comparison: at a large employer quits followed peers' raises more than the market and hardly moved when raises were similar, and each market-rate hire resets the comparison. At the unit level turnover lowers customer satisfaction and productivity at about −.22 and profit margin at −.15, and the practices that hold a unit together are firm-specific training (−.40), internal mobility (−.25) and selective hiring, with staffing levels, straight pay and general training not significant. The replacement cost the case studies measure is about a fifth of salary; the 90–200 percent multiples name no study. The step up a small firm can offer is the one the evidence warns about (promoting on output costs 7.5 percent of manager value added per doubling of prior sales). The arrangements an onsite cleared job cannot offer are worth 8 to 20 percent of pay and a long commute adds ten points to the chance of moving.
